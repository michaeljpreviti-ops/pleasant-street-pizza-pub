Accessibility

Our commitment to an accessible website

We want everyone to be able to see our menu, read our hours and place an order — including people who use screen readers, navigate by keyboard, enlarge text, or rely on captions and high contrast.

The standard we build to

The Americans with Disabilities Act does not set a technical standard for websites, and no one can "certify" a site as ADA compliant. What the Department of Justice points to is the Web Content Accessibility Guidelines (WCAG), and its 2024 rule for state and local governments adopts WCAG 2.1 Level AA. We build this site to that same level, and we follow the plain-language practices the DOJ publishes at ada.gov.

As a restaurant open to the public, we are a "public accommodation" under Title III of the ADA. We treat accessibility as ongoing work, not a box we ticked once.

What the DOJ asks for, and what we did

These are the practices listed in the DOJ's web accessibility guidance, in their order, with what we do about each.

  1. Keep enough contrast between text and background

    Every text and background pair on the site was measured. All of them meet or beat the 4.5:1 ratio WCAG asks for normal text — most are well above it. The headline over the storefront photo sits on a darkened overlay, and the page also sets a dark fallback colour behind it so the text stays readable if the photo never loads.

  2. Don't rely on colour alone

    Nothing on the site is communicated by colour by itself. The star ratings carry a text label as well as the gold stars — a screen reader announces "4.8 out of 5 stars". Hours say "Closed" in words rather than greying a row out.

  3. Write short, descriptive alt text for images

    Every photograph has alt text describing what is in it. Decorative items — the arrows on the ordering links, the phone icon, the logo monogram — are hidden from screen readers so they are not read out as noise.

  4. Add accurate captions to videos

    There is no video or audio on this site. If we add any, it will ship with captions.

  5. Label form fields and explain errors clearly

    This site has no forms. Ordering happens on Toast or 352 Delivery — see the known limitations below — and you can always order by phone instead.

  6. Support browser zoom and text resizing

    The layout is built in relative units, so it reflows rather than breaks. It stays usable at 200% zoom and down to a 320-pixel-wide screen without side-to-side scrolling, and it survives increased letter, word and line spacing.

  7. Build proper headings into the page layout

    One H1, then H2s for each section and H3s beneath them, in order and never skipped. The page uses real landmarks — header, navigation, main, footer — so screen reader users can jump straight to a section. The hours are a real data table with row headers.

  8. Support keyboard navigation, not just the mouse

    Everything you can click, you can reach with the Tab key and activate with Enter, in the order it appears on screen. A "Skip to content" link is the first thing you reach, and whatever has keyboard focus is marked with a thick two-tone ring that stays visible on both the light and the dark parts of the page.

  9. Use automated checkers and manual review

    The DOJ is explicit that a clean automated report does not mean a site is accessible. We ran WAVE by WebAIM, then went through the page by hand: tabbing the whole page, checking it at 200% zoom and at phone width, and measuring every colour pair against the WCAG formula rather than trusting a glance.

  10. Give people a way to report accessibility problems

    Below, and on every page in the footer.

Other things we did

  • Motion is reduced automatically if your device asks for that.
  • The map is in a labelled frame, so it is announced rather than appearing as an unnamed region.
  • Links say where they go — no bare "click here" — and the ones that leave the site are marked.
  • Tap targets are large enough to hit comfortably, and the phone number is a single tap on a phone.
  • The page is marked as English so screen readers use the right pronunciation.

What we know still needs work

We would rather tell you this than let you run into it.

  • The ordering pages are not ours. "Order Now" hands you to Toast, and "Order Delivery" to 352 Delivery. Those are run by other companies and we do not control how accessible they are. If either one gets in your way, call us at (352) 505-5777 and we will take your order on the phone, at the same prices.
  • The map is Google's. The embedded map comes from Google Maps. Our address is written out in text right next to it, so you never need the map to find us.
  • Review text is quoted as written. Guest reviews are reproduced from Google in the reviewer's own words, so spelling and punctuation are theirs.

Tell us about a problem

If any part of this site — or anything at the restaurant — is hard to use, we want to hear about it. Tell us what you were trying to do and what got in the way, and we will fix it or find you another way to get it done.
